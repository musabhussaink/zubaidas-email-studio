# The Steep Club launch plan

**Brand:** Kettlebird Tea (the made-up practice brand)
**Campaign:** Launch The Steep Club, a monthly tea subscription box, to our waitlist
**Dates:** Monday 7 December 2026 to Sunday 3 January 2027 (4 weeks)
**Channel:** email only, no paid ads
**Status:** draft plan. Nothing is sent or scheduled from this studio.

> **About the voice:** `brand/voice.md` is written for Zubaidas, not Kettlebird Tea. This plan follows its general rules (plain English, warm tone, no fake urgency, no claims we can't prove). The baby-specific parts don't apply. If Kettlebird becomes a real brand, it needs its own voice guide.

---

## 1. Campaign overview

**Name:** *Your first steep*

**In one sentence:** Over four weeks, we invite the 2,000 people on our waitlist to join The Steep Club, with emails written for each of the three groups who signed up.

**Main goal:** 300 waitlist members start a subscription by Sunday 3 January 2027. That's 15% of the waitlist.

**Other goals:**
- Learn which of the three groups joins most, so later campaigns can focus on them.
- Keep unsubscribes low, so people who don't join now are still on our list for later.
- Get some early gift subscriptions in time for the December holidays.

**A note on the target:** 15% is a high conversion rate for a launch that only uses email. It's possible because these people asked to hear from us, but we should track it weekly (see section 7) and not fall back on pressure tactics if we're behind.

---

## 2. Target audience

About 2,000 people who joined the waitlist. They already know about The Steep Club and want to hear more, so they're at the **decision stage**: we don't need to explain who we are, just help them say yes.

| Group | Who they are | What they want | What might stop them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tea-curious beginners** | Interested in tea but unsure where to start | An easy, friendly way to try new teas without wasting money on ones they won't like | Feeling they don't know enough, worrying about how to brew, not sure it's worth the price |
| **Gift buyers** | Want a thoughtful present for someone who loves tea | A gift that feels personal and keeps arriving, easy to buy and send | Delivery timing, whether they can choose the start date, how the gift is presented, price |
| **Daily tea drinkers** | Already drink tea every day | Better or more varied tea than the supermarket, without the effort of hunting for it | Already have favourite teas, think it costs more than their usual tea, worry they'll be stuck in a subscription |

**Where they are:** their inbox. We know all three groups read email because they signed up through it.

**Before we start:** we need to know which group each person belongs to. If the waitlist form didn't ask, the first email can ask them to pick (see section 5).

---

## 3. Key messages

**Main message:** *A month of good tea, chosen for you and delivered to your door, and you can skip or cancel anytime.*

(Only use "skip or cancel anytime" if the subscription really works that way.)

**Supporting messages:**

1. **Discovery made easy.** Every box brings teas you might not find on your own, with simple brewing notes. *Proof needed: what's actually in a box, and a sample brewing card.*
2. **A gift that keeps arriving.** One gift, a new box every month. *Proof needed: gift options, whether buyers can pick a start date, and real delivery dates.*
3. **Your daily cup, with more variety.** Rotate in new teas alongside your favourites. *Proof needed: tea list, amounts per box, price per cup if we can work it out honestly.*
4. **No risk of being stuck.** Clear pricing, easy to skip or cancel. *Proof needed: the real subscription terms.*

**Message by group:**
- **Beginners:** reassuring and teaching. "You don't need to know anything about tea to start."
- **Gift buyers:** practical and warm. Lead with how it works as a gift and when it arrives.
- **Daily drinkers:** confident and specific. Lead with the teas, the variety and the value.

**Claims we must not make** (from the voice guide's rules):
- No health claims about tea, such as "detox", "boosts immunity", "helps you sleep" or "aids weight loss".
- No "organic", "ethically sourced", "rare" or "hand-picked" unless we can prove it for that tea.
- No "only X spots left" or "last chance" unless that's really true.
- No "best tea subscription" or similar.

## 4. Channel strategy

**Email only.** This is the right fit: the audience is a list that opted in, the budget is small, and email costs almost nothing per message.

| What | Why it fits | Format | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Launch emails** | Reaches the whole waitlist directly | Short, image-light emails with one call to action | Medium (8 emails, with versions for each group) |
| **Signup page** | Every email needs somewhere to send people | One page that explains the box, price and terms, with a sign-up button | Medium, and must be ready before Monday 7 December |
| **Welcome email for new members** | Confirms the sign-up and stops further launch emails | One email | Low |

**Rules for the sends:**
- Once someone subscribes, they **stop getting launch emails** and get the welcome email instead.
- Each person gets **no more than two emails a week**.
- Every email links to the same signup page, with a tag showing which email and which group it came from, so we can see what works.

## 7. Success metrics

**Main measure:** new subscriptions from the waitlist. **Target: 300 by Sunday 3 January 2027.**

**Weekly pace to stay on track** (a guide, not a rule; launches usually start strongest):

| By end of | Total subscriptions |
|---|---|
| Week 1 (Sun 13 Dec) | 120 |
| Week 2 (Sun 20 Dec) | 200 |
| Week 3 (Sun 27 Dec) | 250 |
| Week 4 (Sun 3 Jan) | 300 |

**Other measures:**

| Measure | Why it matters | How we track it |
|---|---|---|
| Subscriptions by group | Shows which group to focus on next | Group tag on each signup link |
| Click rate per email | Shows which messages work | Email platform reports |
| Signup page conversion | Shows whether the page or the emails are the problem | Visits to the page vs. sign-ups |
| Unsubscribe rate | Warns us if we're emailing too much | Email platform reports. Pause and review if one email gets far more unsubscribes than the others. |
| Gift subscriptions | Shows whether gifting is worth more effort | Gift option on the signup page |

**When to check:**
- **Twice a week:** quick look at sign-ups after each send.
- **Every Monday:** compare against the weekly pace and adjust the next week's emails.
- **Mon 4 Jan:** final results, what worked, and what to do with people who didn't join.

The budget is small and email only, so most of the cost is time rather than money.

| Where it goes | Notes |

No money goes on ads.

## 9. Risks and how to handle them

| Risk | What we'd do |
|---|---|
| **We're behind the weekly pace.** 15% is ambitious. | Check whether the problem is the emails (low clicks) or the signup page (clicks but no sign-ups) and fix that. Send an extra email to the slowest group. Don't add fake urgency. |
| **Some of the waitlist has gone cold**, especially people who joined a long time ago. | Make E1 clearly remind them they asked to hear from us. Watch for bounces and complaints, and stop emailing people who never open. |
| **We get more sign-ups than we can pack and ship.** | Confirm before launch how many boxes we can fulfil in December. If we hit the limit, pause the emails, don't oversell. |
| **December holidays mean busy inboxes**, and gift delivery might arrive late. | Send early in the week. Only state delivery dates we can keep. Offer the printable gift note for later starts. |
| **We don't know which group people are in.** | Ask in E1. Send the "everyone" version of E2 to those who don't answer. |
